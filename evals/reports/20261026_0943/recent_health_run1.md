**Malaria Vaccine Rollout in Africa – 2026 Update**

*Across the continent, malaria vaccines have moved from pilot projects to routine childhood immunisation in a growing number of countries. By early 2026, 25 African nations are delivering the vaccine, with dose deliveries and programme expansions accelerating sharply, yet recent spikes in malaria cases highlight ongoing vulnerabilities.*  

---  

### 1. Scope of the Rollout  

- **Countries covered:** As of April 2026, the WHO reports that **25 African countries** are implementing malaria vaccines, including **nine of the ten highest‑burden nations** [1]. Gavi’s data confirm the same count, noting that **25 endemic countries** have introduced the vaccine into routine immunisation programmes with Gavi support as of 28 January 2026 [2].  
- **New introductions:** In 2026, **Guinea‑Bissau** became the latest country to add the vaccine [2]; seven additional countries (Burundi, Uganda, Mali, Guinea, Togo, Ethiopia, Zambia) started in 2025 [2]; and fourteen countries (e.g., Cameroon, Burkina Faso, Sierra Leone, Benin, Liberia, Côte d’Ivoire) began in 2024 [2].  

### 2. Scale of Vaccine Distribution  

- **Dose volumes:** In 2025, **28.3 million doses** were distributed across the continent, a **169 % increase** over the previous year [1]. Gavi reports that **over 39 million doses** have been delivered to the 25 countries by January 2026 [2][4].  
- **Target population:** The WHO notes that **more than 10 million children each year** are targeted for vaccination in these countries [3]. Gavi projects an additional **50 million children** to be immunised between 2026‑2030, potentially averting **≈170 000 deaths** [4].  

### 3. Geographic and Epidemiological Context  

- **High‑burden focus:** The rollout includes the majority of the continent’s highest‑burden settings, with nine of the ten worst‑affected countries now vaccinating widely [1].  
- **Recent case surges:** Despite vaccine expansion, some regions are experiencing sharp rebounds in malaria transmission. Namibia reported **2.5 ×** the cases in the first three months of 2026 compared with all of 2024, and Mozambique saw **>4 ×** the cases in the same 2026 period versus the previous year [1]. These spikes illustrate how quickly gains can erode when control measures are strained.  

### 4. Impact, Funding, and Remaining Gaps  

- **Health impact:** Early evidence indicates the vaccine reduces hospitalisations and child deaths, as observed in northwestern Nigeria [4]. WHO’s 2026 statement emphasizes solid evidence that the vaccine can change child‑mortality trajectories [3].  
- **Financing needs:** WHO officials stress that **demand is high and supply sufficient**, but **additional financing** is required for countries to purchase enough vaccine and integrate it with other malaria tools [3]. Gavi’s equitable‑pricing deal aims to protect **7 million more children** by the end of the decade [4].  

### 5. Limitations  

- The data are largely drawn from **WHO, Gavi, and vaccine‑focused publications**, which may present a **programme‑centric perspective** and rely on reported dose deliveries rather than independent coverage surveys.  
- **Epidemiological outcomes** (e.g., case reductions) are only briefly mentioned for a few countries; broader impact assessments across all 25 nations are not provided in the sources.  
- The **spike in cases** reported for Namibia and Mozambique is anecdotal and not linked to vaccine coverage levels, limiting conclusions about vaccine effectiveness in those settings.

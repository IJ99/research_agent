**Cash‑less Policy and Small Businesses in Nigeria – Updated Assessment**

*The Central Bank of Nigeria’s cash‑less initiative aims to modernise payments, lower banking costs and broaden financial inclusion. Evidence on how the policy has actually affected small‑business performance remains limited, drawing mainly from a handful of state‑level studies and broader policy‑level analyses.*  

---  

### 1. Policy Overview  
The CBN’s cash‑less policy is described as a “package of measures” intended to (i) modernise the payment system in line with Vision 2020, (ii) reduce the cost of banking services and expand financial inclusion, and (iii) improve monetary‑policy effectiveness for inflation control and growth [1]. The relaunched initiative also seeks to curb financial crime, tax avoidance and cash dependency while promoting digital financial services [2].  

### 2. Intended Benefits for Small Enterprises  
Policy documents and academic commentary argue that moving to digital payments should enhance transparency and efficiency for Small and Medium‑Scale Enterprises (SMEs) [4]. The CBN’s stakeholder‑engagement programme explicitly includes market groups, professional bodies and other associations to raise awareness of the policy’s implications for businesses [7].  

### 3. Empirical Evidence of Impact  

| Study | Scope | Main Findings |
|-------|-------|---------------|
| **Effect of Cashless Policy on Small Business Performance** – Bayelsa State | Quantitative analysis of small‑business enterprises in Bayelsa | Shows “substantial connections” between the Naira‑note redesign component of the cash‑less policy and small‑business performance, indicating that the redesign affects outcomes such as cash availability [5]. |
| **Cashless Policy Impact on SMEs in Bauchi** | Survey of SMEs in Bauchi Metropolis | Finds that the cash‑less policy positively influences SME performance, while also highlighting challenges such as limited access to digital payment infrastructure [8]. |
| **Socio‑economic considerations** – national overview | Qualitative assessment of infrastructural, exclusionary and cost implications | Points to potential challenges for businesses, especially where digital infrastructure is weak, suggesting that the policy could impose additional costs on small firms [2]. |

The limited empirical work suggests that the cash‑less policy **does influence** small‑business performance, but the direction of the effect is mixed: digitalisation can improve transaction security and record‑keeping, while constraints such as cash scarcity and inadequate digital infrastructure may hinder operations.  

### 4. Challenges and Considerations  

* **Infrastructure gaps** – The policy faces infrastructural challenges [2].  
* **Cash scarcity** – The Naira‑note redesign component has been reported to create artificial scarcity of cash, disrupting cash‑dependent transactions for small enterprises [5].  

### 5. Limitations  

* Empirical evidence is confined to a few state‑level studies (Bayelsa, Bauchi) and does not provide a comprehensive national picture [5][8].  
* Several sources (e.g., [4]) present policy intentions and vendor‑type claims without independent outcome data.

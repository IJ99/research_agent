**Malaria Vaccine Rollout in Africa – 2024‑2026 Update**  

*By late 2026, 25 African countries have introduced the RTS,S/AS01 malaria vaccine into routine immunisation programmes, delivering over 39 million doses. Early impact data from Cameroon and Burkina Faso show measurable reductions in malaria cases, while logistical and health‑system constraints continue to shape the pace of expansion.*  

---  

### 1. Geographic Reach and Timeline  

- **Countries covered** – As of 28 January 2026, 25 African nations have incorporated the malaria vaccine into their routine schedules with Gavi support [4].  
- **First introductions** – Cameroon launched the vaccine in January 2024, starting with 42 districts and becoming the earliest adopter [2].  
- **2024 expansion** – Fourteen additional countries introduced the vaccine for the first time in 2024, including Cameroon, Burkina Faso, Sierra Leone, Benin, Liberia, Côte d’Ivoire, South Sudan, Mozambique, Central African Republic, Niger, Chad, Democratic Republic of Congo, Sudan, and Nigeria [1][2].  
- **DRC provincial rollout** – In October 2024 the Democratic Republic of the Congo began a rollout in Kongo‑Central province, targeting >170 000 children per year [1].  
- **Dose delivery** – Over 39 million vaccine doses had been shipped to the 25 participating countries by 28 January 2026 [4].  

### 2. Early Impact Evidence  

- **Cameroon** – Surveillance data documented a 17 % decline in laboratory‑confirmed malaria cases between 2024 and 2025 after vaccine introduction [3].  
- **Burkina Faso** – Total malaria cases fell from 10.8 million to 7.3 million (‑32 %) and a 38 % reduction was observed among children under five following the February 2024 rollout [3].  
- **Interpretation** – Scientists note that the vaccine’s life‑saving impact is maximised when combined with effective antimalarial treatment and other control measures [3].  

### 3. Implementation Challenges  

- **Logistical barriers** – Rural, malaria‑endemic regions often lack reliable road networks and cold‑chain capacity, hindering vaccine distribution [7].  
- **Health‑system capacity** – Overburdened facilities and limited staffing can delay vaccine administration, especially in remote districts [7].  
- **Competing health priorities** – The DRC rollout proceeded despite simultaneous responses to an mpox outbreak, illustrating the need to balance multiple public‑health emergencies [1].  
- **Programmatic support** – Gavi and partners such as the Sabin Vaccine Institute are testing outreach innovations (e.g., the RE‑AIM framework) to improve reach and sustainability; preliminary findings are expected later in 2026 [5].  

### 4. Outlook & Next Steps  

- **Scale‑up outlook** – Ongoing efforts aim to extend routine use to additional malaria‑endemic African nations, building on the evidence and implementation lessons gathered so far.  
- **Monitoring and evaluation** – WHO and Oxford experts discuss implementation results, programme demand, rollout challenges, and next‑generation vaccine strategies in a 2025 video presentation [6].  
- **Vaccine pipeline** – A second malaria vaccine, R21/Matrix‑M, is in development and could be WHO‑prequalified in the near future, potentially expanding the portfolio of tools available for malaria control [8].  

### 5. Limitations  

- Quantitative impact data are currently limited to a few early‑adopter countries (Cameroon, Burkina Faso); continent‑wide effectiveness metrics are not yet available.  
- Several sources (e.g., Gavi’s programme page and Sabin’s workshop description) are promotional in nature; they provide factual counts of countries and doses but do not independently verify coverage quality.  
- Real‑world monitoring and impact estimates are still evolving, and supply‑chain assessments remain incomplete.
